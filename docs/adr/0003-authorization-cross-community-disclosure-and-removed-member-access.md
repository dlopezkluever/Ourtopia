# Authorization, cross-community disclosure, and removed-member access

An active Community Member may read ordinary content in that Community. An administrator may perform that Community's membership, moderation, vote, and Dispute duties but gains no general access to private conversations. Exchange and Dispute participants may read their own records and evidence. A suspended or removed member cannot browse ordinary Community content or start Listings, Need Posts, Borrow Requests, conversations, messages, or votes. The participant exception covers an Exchange or Dispute linked to that person before suspension or removal, plus a Dispute opened later about that Exchange within the existing 30-day reporting window. It permits the record, its necessary schedule and evidence, and structured acknowledgments, confirmations, issue reports, and Dispute responses needed to resolve the obligation. For an eligible Dispute, the person may read existing messages in a conversation with the Exchange counterparty to select evidence but cannot send new messages. Access to a completed Exchange ends 30 days after completion unless a Dispute remains open; then it ends when that Dispute closes. Access to another open obligation ends when its Exchange and Disputes close. The person retains access to their own reputation history. After removal, a new membership request requires a new Invite and decision.

Base Community rows remain private under Row Level Security. A member assessing another person with whom they share an active Community receives a separate redacted global trust result: `globalScore`, `completedInteractions`, and an `incidents` list containing only `type` and `status`. That result contains no source Community identifier or name, source record ID, participants, evidence, or messages. Detailed events about another person require an active shared Community; a person may inspect their own events across past memberships. The server checks the viewer and returns only these fields rather than granting cross-Community access to source rows.

Postgres policies and server commands apply the same actor rules. Storage objects remain in private buckets with record-linked access rules. A server authorizes each short-lived signed URL before issuing it; the URL is a bearer credential and never appears in a public feed or notification. Realtime sends record IDs or notification hints, not private message or evidence bodies. Clients fetch content through a fresh authorized read. Database change subscriptions apply row policies, and membership changes force a client authorization refresh and clear cached Community data. The narrow obligation channel remains available. A test must read rows, objects, and subscriptions as each actor, including after a membership change.
